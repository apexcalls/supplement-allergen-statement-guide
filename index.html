Reading a Supplement's Allergen Statement

Supplements rarely cause allergic reactions — but when they do, the culprit is usually hiding in the allergen statement, not the Supplement Facts panel. Here is how to read it properly.

The allergen statement is the "Contains:" line, usually printed near the ingredients. In the US, manufacturers must declare the major food allergens: milk, eggs, fish, shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soy and sesame. This declaration is separate from the ingredient list, which means an allergen can appear in the "Contains" line even if it is not obvious from the ingredients.

The trickier part is cross-contact. Many facilities handle multiple products, and a brand may add "manufactured in a facility that also processes soy, milk and tree nuts" as a precaution. This is not an admission of contamination — it is a disclosure of shared equipment. Whether it matters depends on your sensitivity level.

Also watch the "free from" claims. "Gluten-free" has a regulated meaning; "allergen-free" does not. A product can be gluten-free and still contain soy or dairy derivatives, so always read the full statement rather than stopping at the reassuring headline.
